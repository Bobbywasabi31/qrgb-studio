2 · Read

PNG decodes directly. With the camera, tap Live scan and point at the code — it finds the three colored finder centers (red, green, blue) on its own and decodes when it can; hold steady and let Heavy codes do the rest. For uploaded photos instead, tap the four outer grid corners in this order: top-left → top-right → bottom-right → bottom-left. How this works

Every data module represents one of five symbols (base 5). Each group of 14 modules stores 4 bytes. The three color-coded finder markers define orientation. Version 2 encodes data with Reed–Solomon RS(255,239), correcting up to eight arbitrary erroneous bytes per codeword, then verifies the CRC-32. Version 3 keeps that framing and stores the original filename (up to 255 bytes) between the header and the payload, inside the same error correction. Version 4 adds a Heavy level — RS(255,127), correcting up to 64 erroneous bytes per codeword at about half the capacity — flagged in the header and detected automatically by the reader. Photos use a four-point projective transform selected by the user.

This is a new QRGB format, not a standard QR code. Ordinary phone QR readers cannot scan it. v2 adds byte-level error correction, sampled color calibration, and manually guided perspective correction. It cannot guarantee reliable results from low-resolution photos; print and camera testing are still needed. Original v1, v2, and v3 PNGs remain readable.
